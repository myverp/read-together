# The Space Between Pages

## A small invitation

Mara found the book on the kitchen table, beside two cups that did not match. One was blue, the other green. A slip of paper rested between the first pages. It said, “Read a little with me.” There was no deadline and no instruction to start at the same time.

Outside, rain drew thin lines on the window. Inside, the kettle made the familiar sound of a room preparing to become quiet. Mara opened the book. Across town, her friend Eli was opening the same story at a desk beneath a bright lamp. They could share a story without sharing a table.

Mara read slowly. She liked the way a sentence could change when she returned to it. Eli read in quick bursts, between the small tasks of an ordinary evening. Sometimes their places were close. Sometimes several pages lay between them. Neither pace was a rule for the other.

The first sentence Mara wanted to remember was simple: “A good companion leaves room for your own steps.” She marked it, then wrote a small note. It reminded her of walking with someone who did not hurry her, even when the path was familiar.

Eli found the mark later. He added a thought of his own. The story now held a conversation that could wait for both people. A reply did not have to arrive immediately to matter. Mara set down her cup and turned the page.

## The path beside the river

On Saturday they met beside the river. Eli carried bread in a paper bag, and Mara carried no plan. The path passed a row of narrow houses, a patch of wild grass, and a bench with one loose board. They chose the bench carefully and watched a duck investigate the empty space between their shoes.

“Did you reach the part about the garden?” Eli asked. Mara shook her head. She had stopped at a description of a room with an open window. She liked that room, although it existed only in words. Eli smiled and kept the garden to himself for a while.

They talked about the sentence Mara had marked. Eli had read it as an invitation to explore. Mara had read it as permission to pause. Both meanings fit. The book had offered one sentence, and they had brought two evenings to it.

The river moved at its own pace. A cyclist rang a bell, a child counted birds, and somewhere a dog objected to the rain. Mara traced a little boat in the margin of her notebook. Eli drew a larger boat beside it. Neither drawing was especially good. That was part of its charm.

On the walk home they chose another short chapter to read. They agreed to stop wherever they wanted. If one reached the end first, the other could still take the long way through the words. The conversation would be there when they returned.

## Returning to the page

That evening Eli opened the book where he had left it. The lamp was still bright, but the desk was tidier. Mara opened hers after dinner. Her blue cup was empty now, and the rain had moved on. Each found a familiar place inside a different room.

Mara reached the garden. It was smaller than she had expected: a few pots, a patient tree, and a chair placed where the morning sun would fall. She understood why Eli had remembered it. The garden asked for attention rather than expertise. Anyone could begin by noticing one leaf.

She marked a phrase and sent him an invitation to look at it. He could come to her page, then return to his own. Sharing a place did not mean losing a place. The thought made her smile. It seemed useful beyond reading, too.

Eli finished the chapter and put the book down. Mara kept reading for another few minutes. When she stopped, she knew where to return. Their small conversation rested inside the story, alongside the marks and the imperfect boats.

There would be other books, other rooms, and evenings when neither felt like reading. For now, a little space between the pages was enough. One story could hold two people, each moving at their own pace, with time to meet again.
